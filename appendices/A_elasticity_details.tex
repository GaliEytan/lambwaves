\chapter{Elasticity algebra and dimensions}\label{app:elasticity}
Using $\dot\epsilon_{ij}=\tfrac12(\dot u_{i,j}+\dot u_{j,i})$ and symmetric
stress, $\sigma_{ij}\dot u_{i,j}=\sigma_{ij}\dot\epsilon_{ij}$. Then
\begin{align}
\dot u_i\sigma_{ij,j}&=(\sigma_{ij}\dot u_i)_{,j}-\sigma_{ij}\dot\epsilon_{ij},\\
\rho\dot u_i\ddot u_i&=\partial_t(\tfrac12\rho\dot u_i\dot u_i),\\
\sigma_{ij}\dot\epsilon_{ij}&=\partial_t(\tfrac12\sigma_{ij}\epsilon_{ij})
\end{align}
for time-independent linear stiffness. These identities give
\cref{eq:energy-balance}. Dimensions are
$[\rho\ddot u]=\si{N.m^{-3}}$, $[e]=\si{J.m^{-3}}$, and
$[S]=\si{W.m^{-2}}$.

Taking divergence of source-free \cref{eq:navier} yields
$\rho\partial_t^2(\nabla\cdot\vect u)=(\lambda+2\mu)\nabla^2(\nabla\cdot\vect u)$;
taking curl eliminates the gradient term and gives
$\rho\partial_t^2(\nabla\times\vect u)=\mu\nabla^2(\nabla\times\vect u)$.
These prove the two potential equations rather than assuming their speeds.
