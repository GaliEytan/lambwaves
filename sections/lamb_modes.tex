\section{Lamb modes and dispersion}

For a harmonic factor $\exp[\ii(kx-\omega t)]$, define the thickness
wavenumbers
\begin{equation}
  p^2=\frac{\omega^2}{c_L^2}-k^2,
  \qquad
  q^2=\frac{\omega^2}{c_T^2}-k^2.
\end{equation}
Applying the traction-free conditions produces the Rayleigh--Lamb equations
\begin{align}
 \frac{\tan(qh)}{\tan(ph)}
 &=-\frac{4k^2pq}{(q^2-k^2)^2}, &&\text{symmetric},\\
 \frac{\tan(qh)}{\tan(ph)}
 &=-\frac{(q^2-k^2)^2}{4k^2pq}, &&\text{antisymmetric}.
\end{align}
Their roots define branches $\omega_n(k)$ and eigenfields $\bm\phi_n(z;k)$.
The corresponding phase and group velocities are
\begin{equation}
  \vph=\frac{\omega_n}{k},\qquad
  \vg=\frac{\dd\omega_n}{\dd k}.
\end{equation}

Near a non-degenerate ZGV point $k_0$,
\begin{equation}
  \omega_n(k)=\omega_0+\frac{D}{2}(k-k_0)^2
  +\mathcal O\!\left((k-k_0)^3\right),
  \qquad D=\omega_n''(k_0).
  \label{eq:zgv-expansion}
\end{equation}
Because $\vg(k_0)=0$, the scale $x/\vg$ is singular there even though the local
resonance remains physically observable.
